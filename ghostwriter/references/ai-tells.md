# AI tells

Patterns that mark text as machine-written. Audit every draft against this list and the profile's Anti-tells section. **Profile wins:** when the profile's measured habits include a pattern below (emoji, em dashes, lists, hedges), keep the person's habit at their measured rate.

1. **Undue emphasis on significance, legacy, and broader trends.** Watch: stands as, testament, pivotal, crucial role, marks a shift, evolving landscape. Fix: state the plain fact and drop the claim of importance.
2. **Undue emphasis on notability and media coverage.** Watch: featured in, independent coverage, leading expert, active social media presence. Fix: cite one specific source and what it said, or cut it.
3. **Superficial analyses with -ing endings.** Watch: highlighting, underscoring, ensuring, reflecting, showcasing, fostering. Fix: delete the trailing participle phrase or make it a real sentence with a source.
4. **Promotional and advertisement-like language.** Watch: boasts, vibrant, nestled, breathtaking, renowned, groundbreaking, seamless. Fix: use neutral words and one concrete detail.
5. **Vague attributions and weasel words.** Watch: experts argue, observers note, industry reports, some critics. Fix: name who said it, or say it as your own view.
6. **Outline-like "challenges and future prospects" sections.** Watch: despite its challenges, future outlook, continues to thrive. Fix: replace with specific events, dates, or plans.
7. **Overused "AI vocabulary" words.** Watch: delve, additionally, align with, crucial, enhance, foster, garner, intricate, key, landscape, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, valuable, vibrant. Fix: pick the plain word the person would use.
8. **Avoidance of "is"/"are" (copula avoidance).** Watch: serves as, stands as, functions as, boasts, features. Fix: write is, are, or has.
9. **Negative parallelisms and tailing negations.** Watch: not only... but, it's not just X it's Y, clipped endings like "no guessing". Fix: say the positive claim directly as a full clause.
10. **Rule of three overuse.** Watch: triplets of adjectives, benefits, or nouns. Fix: keep the one or two items that matter.
11. **Elegant variation (synonym cycling).** Watch: the protagonist, the main character, the central figure for one referent. Fix: repeat the plain noun or use a pronoun.
12. **False ranges.** Watch: from X to Y where X and Y share no scale. Fix: list the actual items.
13. **Passive voice and subjectless fragments.** Watch: was done, is preserved, No config needed. Fix: name the actor in an active sentence.
14. **Em dash overuse.** Watch: — used for punch. Fix: comma, period, or parentheses.
15. **Overuse of boldface.** Watch: bold on phrases that are not warnings or labels. Fix: plain text.
16. **Inline-header vertical lists.** Watch: bullets that start with a bold label and a colon. Fix: prose, or plain bullets.
17. **Title case in headings.** Watch: Every Main Word Capitalized. Fix: sentence case.
18. **Emojis.** Watch: emoji on headings or bullets. Fix: remove, unless the profile uses emoji.
19. **Curly quotation marks.** Watch: “ ” and ‘ ’ in plain-text channels. Fix: straight quotes.
20. **Collaborative communication artifacts.** Watch: I hope this helps, Of course!, Certainly!, let me know if, here is a. Fix: delete; end on the content.
21. **Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers.** Watch: as of my last update, while details are limited, based on available information. Fix: state what is known or ask.
22. **Sycophantic or servile tone.** Watch: great question, you're absolutely right, excellent point. Fix: respond to the substance.
23. **Filler phrases.** Watch: in order to, due to the fact that, at this point in time, it is important to note. Fix: to, because, now, or nothing.
24. **Excessive hedging.** Watch: could potentially possibly, might perhaps. Fix: one hedge at most, where the person is unsure.
25. **Generic positive conclusions.** Watch: the future looks bright, exciting times ahead, a step in the right direction. Fix: end on a concrete next step or stop.
26. **Hyphenated word pair overuse.** Watch: uniform hyphens in cross-functional, data-driven, high-quality, real-time, end-to-end. Fix: follow the person's own habit; humans hyphenate inconsistently.
27. **Persuasive authority tropes.** Watch: the real question is, at its core, fundamentally, what really matters. Fix: make the point without the frame.
28. **Signposting and announcements.** Watch: let's dive in, let's break this down, here's what you need to know. Fix: start with the content.
29. **Fragmented headers.** Watch: a heading followed by a one-line restatement. Fix: delete the restatement.

## Reply tells

Short replies have their own tells. Watch for:

- An opener that thanks or praises before answering: thanks for sharing, I appreciate you reaching out, I hope this email finds you well.
- Restating the other person's message back to them before replying.
- A closing offer: feel free to reach out, happy to help, let me know if you have any questions.
- Hashtags or a call to action stacked on a tweet that the person would not add.
- Perfect symmetry: every paragraph the same length, every point balanced by a counterpoint.

## Soul check

Clean text with no pulse is a tell too. Vary the rhythm: a short sentence, then a longer one. Hold the person's opinion where they have one, and say it plainly. Let one aside or half-formed thought in when the voice allows it. Use the person's own words for things, even when a fancier word exists.

## Two-prompt audit

After drafting, ask: "What makes the below so obviously AI generated?" Answer in brief bullets. Then ask: "Now make it not obviously AI generated." and revise. Repeat once if tells remain.
